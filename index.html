NATIONAL CIVIC INFRASTRUCTURE AUDIT

Locked Out of Play?

Investigating socioeconomic disparities, loose-fill ground barriers, national sanitary catchments, and capital grant targeting across 2,239 Australian public playgrounds and 25,624 public toilets.

ACT I — MACRO & MUNICIPAL PROVISION

The Geography of Play: Capital Clusters & Regional Voids

Across Australia's 2,239 documented OpenStreetMap playgrounds, infrastructure is heavily concentrated along coastal metropolitan belts. Victoria (729 sites), New South Wales (578), and Queensland (486) account for 80.1% of national play spaces, while 64.4% sit within 55 km of state capital CBDs.

Municipal Coverage Across 547 LGAs

Mapping playground counts across Local Government Areas reveals sharp municipal divides. Only 294 of Australia's 547 LGAs have documented play spaces in the civic registry.

Established metropolitan councils—such as Northern Beaches (86 sites), Brisbane (75), and Casey (62)—dominate provision, while rural shires face vast documentation and infrastructure voids.

ACT II — SOCIOECONOMIC EQUITY

The Socioeconomic Divide: ABS SEIFA Deciles & The Regional Gap

Joining LGA playground counts with the ABS Index of Relative Socio-economic Advantage and Disadvantage (SEIFA) exposes a steep socioeconomic gradient between Australia's poorest and wealthiest municipalities.

The 4.7x SEIFA Gap: Disadvantaged councils (Deciles 1–3) average just 1.68 playgrounds per LGA, compared to 7.94 per LGA in advantaged councils (Deciles 8–10).

ACT III — MICRO-LEVEL ASSET QUALITY

Anatomy of Play: Loose-Fill Surfaces, Governance & Age Gaps

For children using wheelchairs or walkers, ground surfacing determines whether a park is inclusive or off-limits. Examining the 423 surface-audited sites in our dataset shows an overwhelming reliance on loose-fill materials.

The Loose-Fill Barrier

Among playgrounds with recorded ground materials, Woodchips & Bark Mulch (44.9%) and Sand (43.5%) make up 88.4% of sites, whereas synthetic Impact Rubber accounts for just 4.3% (18 sites).

Cross-tabulating `surface` against `wheelchair` ratings confirms that loose sand and woodchips disproportionately result in `'no'` or `'limited'` wheelchair access.

ACT IV — NATIONAL ACCESSIBILITY & SANITARY CATCHMENTS

The Dwell-Time Barrier: Wheelchair Audits & National Toilet Co-Location

Inclusive play requires both mobility access and nearby accessible toilets. By linking all 2,239 playgrounds with the 25,624 facilities in the National Public Toilet Map, we evaluate family dwell-time infrastructure across every Australian state.

Among the playgrounds in Australia with a verified `wheelchair` rating, 36% are fully accessible (`yes`), 22% offer `limited` access, and 41% lock out wheelchair users (`no`).

ACT V — EMPIRICAL POLICY CASE STUDY

The Capital Response: NSW 'Everyone Can Play' Grants (140 Projects)

How are governments fixing these deficits? We link all 140 projects ($19.08M) from the NSW Everyone Can Play grant register (2018/19 to 2021/22) to ABS SEIFA council scores to test whether capital funding targeted disadvantaged communities.

Zero Socioeconomic Targeting ($R^2 = 0.0006$)

Grant allocations show virtually zero correlation with council SEIFA scores ($r = 0.025$).

Most strikingly, Decile 1 councils received 0 grants across all four rounds, whereas Decile 10 (the wealthiest 10% of councils) captured 25 grants totaling $3.44M—demonstrating how competitive grant applications favour well-resourced metropolitan councils.
